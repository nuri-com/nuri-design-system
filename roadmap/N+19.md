## 2 · The model in one screen (detail → `resolver-model.md`)

- **Transversal baseline** (theme/factory · §1): four *small, fixed, centralised* vocabularies — **colour** (surface roles, each rest+pressed) · **type** (steps) · **geometry** (space/size/radius) · **interaction** (press-scale/disabled-opacity). Does NOT grow per-consumer.
- **Components consume a subset** of each, independently (§2).
- **Three kinds** (§3): **leaf** (Icon) · **primitive** (open · mechanical subset · *exposes the substrate's interface, never remaps*) · **recipe** (closed · *may remap a subset to a semantic vocabulary*). One rule: remap only to add meaning; expose mechanically otherwise.
- **`surface`** (§4) = the shared colour **data** (a vocabulary), reused via the engine — *not necessarily a component* (open choice · §4). Button/IconButton/IconAvatar/Card consume it; `shape: circle|square` is a real variant modifier.
- **Interaction decomposes** (§5): pressed-**colour** lives in the surface vocab; press-**scale** + disabled-opacity are the interaction baseline, **independent opt-ins**.
- **Format** (§7): **Unistyles-compatible `variants`/`compoundVariants`** — *compatible, not a dependency*; baseline = the `theme`; the engine is a **bounded** custom build on `NuriThemeContext`/`resolveToken`/`typeStyle`.
- **Two scopes** (§12): theme/accent scope (`ScopedTheme`-style · decision 27/63) vs the **surface foreground/label-type context** (propless `<typography>`/`<icon>` inherit). Don't conflate.
- **Worked shapes** (§11): the concrete `theme`, the `Button` API, the emitted bridge.

## 5 · How to work efficiently with the operator (read this)

- **They drive the architecture; you scaffold and pressure-test it.** They think out loud and refine through dialogue. Your job is to ground their ideas in the actual code, surface the real trade-offs, and *challenge* — not to lead them somewhere or rubber-stamp.
- **Be adversarial, not sycophantic.** When they float an idea — often with "I'm probably overcomplicating, but something smells" — they *want* the holes found and named. Validation-as-flattery erodes trust. Tell them straight when something is wrong or oversold (the audit's "kills duplication" overclaim was caught this way).
- **Never presume or approximate — ASK.** Standing instruction: *"non approssimare se non capisci chiedi a me."* They sharply corrected a presumption this session (an injected scope they hadn't agreed to). If unsure what they mean, ask; don't invent scope or advance an unagreed framing.
- **Trust their "smell."** "Are we reinventing?", "is this doable?", "this doesn't feel right" — these are reliable architectural signals every time this session. Dig in; don't wave them off.
- **Hold P11 hard.** They will catch speculative additions and over-engineering (they stripped Button's unproven knobs; corrected "the baseline grows" → "it's small and fixed"). Default to "ship only what has a current consumer."
- **Bring concrete artifacts to confirm.** They lock decisions against *real shapes* — API signatures, data structures, code — not abstract prose. When it matters, show the actual shape and ask them to confirm or adjust.
- **Update your view readily — they do.** They reverse and refine on new evidence and *expect the same back*; several of my leans flipped this session and that was valued, not penalised. Intellectual honesty over consistency.
- **Verify before you relay.** When a subagent/audit returns a load-bearing finding, check it against the code before presenting it as fact (C1 was verified, and held stronger than stated).
- **Delegate execution; reserve your context for decisions, the base, and verification.** The lesson of the prior arc (a coordinator saturated context hand-running mechanical work). Delegate multi-file/mechanical work to a working session, then verify the diff. Document the base *before* you saturate.
- **Manage your budget out loud.** They watch context and resource trade-offs (they prompted this very handoff at 61%). When the next phase is code-heavy and your budget is thinning, say so and hand off cleanly.
- **Style.** Prefer crisp prose + a clear recommendation + one open question over heavy multiple-choice (they answered a structured question with "let's discuss"). They open in Italian, do technical substance in English — keep technical terms/artifacts in English (the codebase language).
